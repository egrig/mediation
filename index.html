A calm, orderly conversation

Mediation is a meeting where a supportive guide helps you talk. We do not decide who is right, and we do not speak for either side. Going to court can be slow, and legal fees add up. Mediation is often quicker and less costly, it keeps the decisions in your hands, and every session happens online, so no one has to travel.

Over 80% of mediations result in mutually-beneficial agreements.

  • Supportive

    We help each person say what matters to them and feel heard. We do not take a side, give legal advice, or act for either person. Our role is to keep the conversation calm and fair and to give each of you time to speak.

  • Private

    Court files are generally open to the public. Mediation is not. Before a session, everyone signs an agreement to mediate, and what you discuss is treated as confidential under it. The law sets some limits, and those are explained before you begin.

  • Your decision

    No one imposes an outcome. You name the questions that need attention, and you shape the answers together. If you reach an understanding, you can write it down. If you do not, nothing is imposed on you.

Conflict resolution policies for organizations

Disputes cost less when everyone knows the process before one starts. We help companies and other organizations set up mediation and negotiation policies: when to raise a concern, how it moves from an informal conversation to mediation, who takes part, and how confidentiality is handled.

We can draft a new policy or review the one you have, working with your HR team. Your own legal counsel should review the final version.

How it works

Four steps, starting with a free conversation and ending with whatever you decide. No one can make you accept an agreement.

  1. 01

    Free consultation

    Tell us a little about the situation and we will set up a free consultation. We explain how mediation works, answer your questions, and help you decide if it fits. There is no cost and no obligation.

  2. 02

    Private intake

    Before anyone meets together, each person talks with the mediator alone. This is the place to raise concerns, say where you feel stuck, and confirm that mediation is safe and suitable.

  3. 03

    Online sessions

    Once everyone is prepared, you meet by video. Each person has time to speak. We keep the conversation respectful and on the questions you came to settle.

  4. 04

    Written agreement

    If you reach an understanding, we can help you write it down. Each person may have their own lawyer review it before anyone signs. If you do not reach agreement, nothing is imposed.

You will not walk into your first joint session cold. By then you will have had your free consultation and a private intake meeting, where we prepare you for the session: how it will run, which questions to start with, and anything you should gather beforehand. The session itself is close to sitting in the same room, without the travel. You can see each other, speak one at a time, and step aside privately with the mediator when you need a break. All you need is a private space, a steady connection, and a device with a camera and microphone.

Common questions

If your question is not here, ask it during the free consultation.

  • What happens in the free consultation?

    We listen to what is going on, explain how mediation works, and answer your questions. Together we decide whether mediation suits your situation. It costs nothing, and you are not committing to anything.

  • What if we barely speak right now?

    Many people come to mediation when talking has become hard. The mediator keeps things calm and gives each person room to be heard. Mediation is not right for everyone, and the private intake is where we check that it is safe for you.

  • What if the other person is not ready?

    You can still book a consultation on your own. Mediation works best when everyone is willing to take part, so we can talk about how to raise the idea with them.

  • How long does it take?

    It depends on how many questions you need to settle and how complicated they are. Some matters wrap up in one session, and others need a few. After intake, we can give you a better sense of what to expect.

  • What if we cannot agree on everything?

    That is all right. You can settle the parts you agree on and set the rest aside. For anything left open, you can get legal advice or try again later.

  • Is what we agree to binding?

    Mediation itself does not bind anyone. If you reach an understanding, it can be put in writing. Whether a signed agreement is legally binding depends on the kind of matter and how it is completed, so each person should get independent legal advice before signing.
